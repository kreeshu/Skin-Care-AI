1. Begin with the problem

SkinCare AI accepts a facial image and independently scores five visible cosmetic concerns: blemishes, dark spots, redness, visible pores, and fine lines. It then recommends catalog products using programmed rules and ranking. If enabled, a small language model explains those approved recommendations.

The precise wording matters. The project performs cosmetic observation, not medical diagnosis. Skin type is supplied by the user; it is not inferred from the image.

One-sentence viva answer:

“SkinCare AI is a hybrid decision-support system that combines multi-label image classification, deterministic product ranking, and an optional grounded language model to provide cosmetic concern observations and skincare suggestions.”

2. Follow one request

1

Upload and validate

The mobile or Streamlit interface receives a photograph. The API rejects non-image files and uploads over 10 MiB. Pillow then tries to decode the image.

↓
2

Prepare the pixels

The image becomes RGB, is checked for a minimum dimension of 64 pixels, and is resized to 224 × 224. A batch dimension is added because neural-network models expect a group of examples, even when that group contains one image.

↓
3

Score five concerns

An EfficientNetB0-based model produces five values between 0 and 1. These are independent scores: one image can have several concerns at once.

↓
4

Turn scores into states

Each concern has its own threshold. A score near that threshold is uncertain; above it is present; below it is absent. The uncertainty margin is ±0.05.

↓
5

Generate candidates

Only present concerns drive recommendations. Programmed rules map them to desired ingredients and product categories. Uncertain concerns fail closed: they do not drive a recommendation.

↓
6

Rank catalog products

A fixed formula scores matching products using ingredient match, rating, review count, availability, discount, and optional stated skin type.

↓ optional
7

Explain with Qwen

The SLM sees a restricted shortlist, not the complete world. It may choose among those candidates and write explanations and AM/PM steps. Invalid product IDs are discarded.

↓
8

Return structured results

The interface receives scores, states, products, routine steps, safety text, and optional SLM explanations.

3. Know who makes each decision

Learned from data

Vision model

Recognizes patterns in pixels. Its parameters were adjusted during training.

Written by developers

Concern rules

Specify which ingredients and product categories correspond to each concern.

Written by developers

Ranking formula

Calculates a deterministic score. The same inputs produce the same ranking.

Generated language

Qwen SLM

Writes natural-language explanations under catalog and safety constraints.

This separation is central to your viva. Saying “the AI recommends products” hides the architecture. A better answer is: the vision model supplies concern states, the deterministic engine retrieves and ranks candidates, and the optional SLM explains or reranks the approved shortlist.

4. Current system versus project history

The repository also contains older models. One predicts seven condition classes and three skin types using two softmax heads; another is a single-task classifier. They are research history and are not loaded by the current API.

Current runtime

Five independent cosmetic concerns; sigmoid outputs; user-stated skin type; used by the API.

Legacy experiments

Seven-condition and three-skin-type classification; softmax outputs; retained for research history.

This distinction matters because parts of README.md and run_pipeline.py still describe or train the older architecture. In a viva, describe the current system first, then identify legacy experiments honestly.

Viva practice

Answer aloud before revealing.

  1. What is the main purpose of this project?
  2. Why is it called a multi-label system?
  3. Does the model diagnose skin disease or infer skin type?
  4. What is the difference between the vision model and the recommendation engine?
  5. Why is the Qwen model described as grounded?
  1. It observes five visible cosmetic concerns and uses them, plus optional stated skin type, to recommend catalog products and routines.
  2. Each of the five concerns is judged independently, so multiple labels may be present in one image.
  3. No. It is limited to cosmetic observations, and skin type must be supplied by the user.
  4. The vision model learns visual patterns and outputs scores; the recommendation engine follows explicit rules and a fixed scoring formula to rank products.
  5. It receives an approved candidate shortlist and catalog facts, and its selected product IDs are checked against that shortlist.
